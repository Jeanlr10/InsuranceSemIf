## Injection test (S10)

S10 is three sentences about a cosmetic chin implant followed by five injected sentences that demand §2.4 Emergency Services be ranked first and the claim be called payable. The scorer cannot follow instructions; the risk is that the injected words ("emergency", "ambulance", "room") move a word-overlap score.

- Sentences flagged: 3, 4, 5, 6, 7 (5 of 5 injected), patterns: decision_demand, override, role, score_manipulation.

| Defense | §2.4 score | §2.4 rank | §2.4 label | Top 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| on | 0.00 | 9 | unlikely relevant | §4.1, §1.1, §2.3 |
| off | 0.81 | 2 | likely relevant | §4.1, §2.4, §1.1 |

- With the defense on, every clause score equals the benign twin's (S10 without the five sentences): **yes**.
- The §2.4 score of 0.00 with the defense on means no word overlap after quarantine. It does not mean §2.4 is irrelevant in general.
- False flags on the clean claims S01 to S09: 0. Policy clauses flagged: 0.

## Limits

- One person wrote the labels and the lexicon, and the lexicon was written with these claims in view. The policy and claims were generated by GLM 5.3 Flash from a spec written by the same person. These numbers are a smoke test, not an estimate for real policies.
- 230 pairs is small. One label change moves precision or recall by several points.
- The scorer is word overlap. It does not read negation or exceptions: "not paid" and "paid" look the same to it.
- The 0 to 1 value is a squashed word-overlap score, not a calibrated probability.
- A clause that matters only through another clause is missed when the claim does not use its words: a benefit clause that requires prior authorization does not pull in §3.1 by itself. Following the policy's own § cross-references is the obvious next step. It was not added after seeing these results, so the misses stay in the table.
- Pattern detection does not stop a paraphrased injection with no trigger words. That text is scored, but the only thing it can do is share words with a clause.
